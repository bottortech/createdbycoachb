## Traditional Homework Will Become Obsolete

For generations, homework has been one of the primary ways schools measured learning.

Students took worksheets home, completed essays, solved math problems, and answered questions independently. The assumption was simple: if students completed the work themselves, teachers could evaluate what they understood.

**Artificial intelligence changes that assumption.**

---

## Why Homework No Longer Proves Understanding

Today, a student can ask AI to explain algebra, summarize a chapter, write an essay, or solve complex problems in seconds. As these tools become more capable, it becomes increasingly difficult to know whether homework reflects a student's understanding or an AI's output.

Because of that, I believe traditional homework will gradually become obsolete.

This doesn't mean learning disappears. It means the way schools measure learning will change.

---

## From Worksheets to Demonstration

Instead of assigning more take-home worksheets, educators will place greater emphasis on activities that require students to demonstrate understanding directly.

Students may build prototypes instead of writing reports. They may conduct science experiments instead of answering textbook questions. They may explain concepts in person, collaborate on projects, create presentations, perform demonstrations, or solve real-world problems in the classroom.

**AI will become a learning partner, not simply a shortcut.**

Students will still use AI to study, ask questions, and receive personalized explanations. But the final assessment will increasingly focus on what they can demonstrate, apply, and create.

---

## A Familiar Shift

We've seen similar shifts before.

Calculators changed math instruction. Search engines changed research. The internet changed access to information.

AI will change how schools evaluate learning.

The goal of education has never been to complete worksheets. The goal has always been to develop knowledge, critical thinking, creativity, and problem-solving. As AI becomes more capable, schools will return to measuring those skills in ways that technology cannot easily replace.
