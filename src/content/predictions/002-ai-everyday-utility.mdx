## AI Will Become an Everyday Utility

Every major technology follows a familiar pattern.

At first, people debate whether it should exist. Then they slowly begin using it. Eventually, it becomes so common that nobody thinks about it anymore.

**Artificial intelligence is following that exact path.**

---

## The Pattern Technology Always Follows

Think about calculators. When they first became popular, many people believed students would never learn math if calculators were allowed in classrooms. Today, calculators are simply another tool.

The same thing happened with search engines. There was a time when looking up information meant visiting a library or flipping through an encyclopedia. Today, typing a question into a search bar feels completely normal.

GPS followed the same path. People once carried printed maps and memorized directions. Today, most people don't even think about navigation — they simply open an app and drive.

Smartphones combined dozens of devices into one. Cameras, flashlights, calculators, calendars, music players, alarm clocks, and maps all became features instead of separate products.

**AI is next.**

---

## What Comes Next for AI

Right now, many people intentionally open AI applications like ChatGPT or Claude. In the future, that behavior will become less common because AI won't be a separate destination — it will simply exist inside the products people already use.

Your email will write drafts before you ask.

Your calendar will reorganize your schedule automatically.

Your phone will summarize conversations.

Your car will help plan routes based on traffic, charging, or weather.

Your computer will explain documents, generate presentations, and organize files with little effort from you.

At some point, people will stop saying they're "using AI." They'll simply say they're sending an email, creating a presentation, planning a trip, or finishing work.

**The AI will be invisible.**

---

## The Invisible Threshold

History suggests that the technologies with the greatest impact eventually become the least noticeable. Electricity transformed society, but nobody says they're "using electricity" when they turn on a light.

I believe artificial intelligence will follow that same path.

The future of AI isn't that everyone talks about it.

The future is that almost nobody does — because it's quietly powering the tools people use every day.
