## AI Will Transform Accessibility

Artificial intelligence will transform accessibility by reducing the barriers that prevent people with disabilities from independently participating in everyday life.

---

## Why I Believe This

For generations, people with disabilities have had to adapt to a world that wasn't necessarily designed for them.

I believe we are entering an era where technology will increasingly adapt to the individual instead.

Historically, assistive technology has been designed to address specific limitations. However, artificial intelligence introduces the opportunity to create intelligent systems that understand and respond to an individual's unique needs.

Imagine a world where:

- Printed books become interactive, accessible learning experiences.
- Individuals with communication difficulties have more personalized ways to express themselves.
- Students receive educational instruction adapted to their individual abilities.
- Individuals with physical disabilities can control their environments through eye movements, voice commands, and other alternative inputs.
- Everyday devices automatically adjust to the accessibility needs of their users.

As a father of a child with autism and a technology founder, accessibility is something I think about beyond its commercial potential. I see opportunities for technology to create greater independence and improve people's quality of life.

I believe AI will help shift accessibility from accommodation toward individual empowerment.

---

## What I Expect to See

Over the next five to ten years, I expect:

- AI-powered assistive technology to become more widely available in schools, libraries, workplaces, and homes.
- More affordable and personalized accessibility solutions.
- Greater integration between AI, robotics, and assistive hardware.
- Accessibility becoming a fundamental consideration in product development rather than an afterthought.
- Increased independence for individuals who traditionally require assistance with everyday activities.

---

## What Would Prove Me Wrong?

If AI-powered accessibility solutions remain prohibitively expensive, fail to deliver meaningful improvements in independence, or struggle to achieve widespread adoption over the next decade, this prediction would need to be reconsidered.

---

## Final Thought

The greatest achievement of artificial intelligence may not be how much faster it allows us to work, but how much more it allows people to do.

The future of accessibility is a world that adapts to people, rather than expecting everyone to adapt to the world.
